You can spot AI-written website copy in a single line.
"We are passionate about delivering high-quality solutions tailored to your needs."

Nothing in it is wrong. It could just as easily belong to a plumber, a dentist or a law firm. Nobody reads it and thinks of you.

AI is useful for website copy. Just not for deciding what to say.

It's much better at organising what your customers have already said. A routine that works:

1. Collect real words. Questions from emails, phrases from reviews, things people ask on the phone.
2. Strip out names and anything that identifies a person before pasting it into any tool.
3. Ask the AI to group them into themes and suggest an order for the page.
4. Write the headlines yourself, in your customers' own wording.
5. Use AI again at the end, only to check clarity and spot gaps.

The page ends up sounding like the business, because it's built from the people who buy from it.

One test for every line on your site: could a competitor paste it onto theirs without changing a word?

If yes, it's not doing any work for you.
