Ask a small business owner who owns their website's domain and there's often a pause.
Sometimes it's in the name of a web designer they haven't spoken to in years.

That's fine until something breaks. The domain expires, email stops working, and nobody has the login to renew it.

Five things that should be in the business's name, not a freelancer's:

1. The domain, with renewal reminders going to an inbox you actually check.
2. The hosting account.
3. An admin login for the website.
4. Your Google Business Profile, with you as owner and anyone else as manager.
5. Analytics, forms and booking tools that hold customer data.

That last one matters under GDPR. If customer messages sit in an account you can't open, you can't answer someone who asks to see or delete their data.

A good handover leaves the client as owner of everything. The designer gets a separate login the client can remove at any time.

Add a password manager and two-factor sign-in on each account, and check it all once a year.

If your web designer vanished tomorrow, which of these could you still get into?
