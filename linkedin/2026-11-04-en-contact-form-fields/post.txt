Most small business contact forms ask for things nobody will ever use.
Date of birth. Full address. A phone number marked "required" when you only ever reply by email.

Every extra field does two things.

It makes people less likely to finish the form.
And it hands you more personal data to look after under GDPR.

The principle has a name: data minimisation. Collect only what you need for the purpose you've told people about.

When I plan a page, I ask one question about every field: "What will you actually do with this?"

No clear answer? It goes.

A check you can do in ten minutes:

1. List every field on your form.
2. Mark the ones you really use to reply or to quote.
3. Delete the rest, or make them optional.
4. Add one line under the button saying what the data is for and how long you keep it.

Do this honestly and most forms shrink to three or four fields: name, a way to reply, and the message.

Shorter form, less risk, and visitors can see you've thought about their data. That's a trust signal that costs nothing.
